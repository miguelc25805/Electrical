# GF Field Command — one-page field guide

Print this. Hand it to a foreman. That's the whole training program.

---

## First time — about ten minutes

1. Open the link. Tap **Add to Home Screen** so it works with no signal.
2. Tap **Open the example job** and poke around for five minutes. Nothing you do
   there touches a real job.
3. Tap the job name (top left) → **New job**, and walk the five setup steps:

   | Step | What to put in |
   |---|---|
   | **Job** | Name, number, GC, county. Mark it public works if it's prevailing wage. |
   | **Crew & wages** | 4×10 or 5×8, and the journeyman base rate off your wage sheet. |
   | **Areas** | Floors, buildings, zones. There are templates — use one. |
   | **Phases** | Eleven standard phases are already there. Delete what this job doesn't have. |
   | **Hours** | Type the estimate's hours where each phase crosses each area. |

You can skip any step and come back. Nothing locks.

---

## Every day — under a minute

**Report** tab → tap the work you put in → enter the quantity (or slide the
percent) → check the crew hours → **Save**.

Yesterday's crew is already filled in. Change what moved.

> Report what's **physically in place**, not how it felt. Hours burned is
> already being tracked separately — that's the whole point.

---

## Every Monday — about ten minutes

- **Crew** tab → check the **Dispatch request** at the bottom. It tells you who
  to call and who to release, by week. Print it or read it to the hall.
- **Plan** tab → **3-week** → anything hatched red is **not ready to work**. Log
  what's in the way in the constraint log and put a name and a date on it.
- **Plan** tab → **Promises** → check off what the crew kept last week, and pick
  a reason for anything missed. That list is the most useful thing on the job.

---

## Every month — about twenty minutes

- **Material** tab → any red card is past the date it had to move. Red on a
  70-week item is a schedule problem, not a paperwork problem.
- **Forecast** tab → check the production factor and where the job lands.
- **Comply** tab (public work) → check the apprentice ratio gauge.

---

## Reading the colors

| | Means |
|---|---|
| ● green | On track |
| ▲ amber | Watch it |
| ■ red | Behind, blocked, or past due |
| diagonal hatching | Not ready to work — something is in the way |

**Production factor** is hours earned ÷ hours spent.
`1.00` is exactly on the estimate. `0.85` means you're burning about 15% more
labor than the estimate allowed.

---

## Three things worth knowing

1. **The apprentice ratio is judged at the end of the job.** One apprentice hour
   for every five journeyman hours, added up across the whole project. If you're
   short in month ten, it's very expensive to fix. The gauge is there so you
   never get surprised.

2. **Long-lead gear has to be scheduled backward.** A pad-mount transformer runs
   over two years. The date that matters isn't when it shows up — it's the last
   day you can still submit it. The app computes that date for you.

3. **Write the delay down the day it happens.** The delays field in the daily
   log, and a T&M ticket signed on the spot, are what turn "we got hammered" into
   a paid change order. An unsigned ticket found at closeout is worth a fraction
   of a signed one.

## Back it up

**Setup** → **Backup** → **Export this job**. Email it to yourself every Friday.

Everything is on your device only — nothing is uploaded anywhere, and nobody
else has a copy unless you send them one.
